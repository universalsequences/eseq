# eseq Manual

eseq combines a step sequencer, playable instruments, effects, and a timeline. Start with a repeating pattern, give individual notes their own sound with parameter locks, and build a song by placing clips or recording a performance.

This first edition covers the graphical music-making workflow on macOS, inspected in September 2026. Packages, custom scripts, custom sequencers, and programming are outside this edition.

- [Concepts and screen tour](concepts) — tracks, patterns, scenes, clips, and panels
- [Your first session](first-session) — make and save a short sequence
- [Sequencer Tour](sequencer-tour) — punch in steps and vary a pattern
- [Instruments and presets](instruments) — add synths and recall sounds
- [Samples and sounds](sample-browser) — find samples and load a sampler
- [Audio effects](effects) — add, bypass, and organize processing
- [MIDI effects](midi-effects) — arpeggiate, repeat, and route notes
- [Instrument racks](racks) — layer sounds and work with drum racks
- [Recording](recording) — arm tracks and play the computer keyboard
- [Parameter locks](parameter-locks) — edit steps, record knob moves, and clear locks
- [Piano roll](piano-roll) — pitches, lengths, chords, and automation
- [Patterns and scenes](patterns-and-scenes) — track variations and project-wide recall
- [Mixer](mixer) — levels, sends, buses, and groups
- [Arrangement](arrangement) — place clips and record patterns, scenes, and takes
- [Saving and audio export](saving-and-export) — preserve a project and make a stereo WAV
- [Navigation and controls](customization) — panels, shortcuts, and manual navigation
- [Troubleshooting](troubleshooting) — silence, recording targets, and unexpected changes

Start with Your first session, or choose the chapter for the task in front of you. A later build may rearrange panels or rename controls; the distinction between patterns, scenes, and takes remains the starting point when a control's effect is unclear.
